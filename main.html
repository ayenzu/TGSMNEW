01 / Explore the channels

One world. Different ways to tell a story.

Each medium has unique strengths and limitations. The format affects how information reaches us, how quickly it changes, and how we respond.

📰

Print Media

Physical text and images created for reading and reference.

NewspapersBooksMagazinesFlyers

Strength: easy to annotate and revisit.

Limitation: may become outdated.

📺

Broadcast Media

Audio and video distributed through established channels.

RadioTelevisionPSAs

Strength: timely audio-visual reporting.

Limitation: editing can shape emphasis.

💻

Digital Media

Information created, stored, or shared electronically.

WebsitesEmailE-booksDatabases

Strength: searchable and easy to update.

Limitation: errors can spread quickly.

🌐

New Media

Interactive, networked media where audiences also create and respond.

Social mediaPodcastsLivestreams

Strength: participation and community voices.

Limitation: virality can amplify misinformation.

🎬

Film Media

Visual stories and information presented through moving images and sound.

MoviesDocumentariesEducational Videos

Strength: Engaging and effective for explaining information visually.

Limitation: May require equipment, electricity, or internet access.

🪧

Outdoor Media

Information displayed in public spaces to reach people in their surroundings.

BillboardsPostersBanners

Strength: Visible to many people and easy to notice in public areas.

Limitation: Limited space for detailed information and may be affected by weather.

For print media, check the publication date and verify facts before believing or sharing information.
For broadcast media, listen carefully, compare reports, and rely on credible news sources.
For new media, think before you click, verify information, and avoid spreading fake news.
For film media, distinguish facts from fiction and check whether documentaries use credible sources.
For outdoor media, read messages critically and verify claims in advertisements or public displays.
For digital media, protect your personal information, use strong passwords, and share content responsibly.
02 / Apply the ideas

Same event, different experiences.

Imagine a typhoon approaching your community. Different media can help—but they do not all play the same role.

Community scenario

A typhoon warning is issued.

Use each channel's strengths, but verify safety instructions with relevant official sources.

📰 PrintNewspapers publish information about the typhoon's location, expected impact, and safety precautions. Printed flyers and brochures are distributed to inform residents about evacuation procedures.
📺 BroadcastTelevision and radio broadcast weather updates, emergency announcements, and evacuation orders to warn the public about the approaching typhoon.
💻 DigitalGovernment agencies send emergency alerts through mobile phones, messaging applications, and digital platforms to provide timely warnings and safety instructions.
🌐 New mediaNews websites and social media platforms share real-time typhoon updates, class suspensions, and emergency announcements.
🎬 Film MediaShort informational videos about typhoon preparedness and evacuation procedures are shown in schools or community centers to educate people about safety.
🪧 Outdoor MediaBillboards and public posters display typhoon warnings, evacuation routes, and emergency hotline numbers in public areas.

What should I trust first?

For emergency instructions, check the relevant official government agency or local government unit and confirm the advisory is current.

What should make me pause?

Undated screenshots, anonymous claims, dramatic wording, missing context, and posts asking you to share immediately.

03 / Think critically

Before you share, run the R.A.V.A.T check.

Five Criteria for Evaluating Resources

R
Reliability

The trustworthiness and accuracy of media and infromation.

A
Accuracy

Verify the names, dates, figures, and places mentioned.

V
Value

The usefulness and importance of media and information in gaining knowledge and making informed decisions.

A
Authority

Identify the author of the content. Know his/her credentials.

T
Timeliness

Look at the date when the material was published.
